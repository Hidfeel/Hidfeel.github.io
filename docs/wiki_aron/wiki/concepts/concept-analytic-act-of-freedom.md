# 分析师的自由行动（act of freedom / x-phenomenon）

> **类型**: concept
> **创建时间**: 2026-08-29
> **最后更新**: 2026-08-29
> **主来源**: [[raw/symington-act-of-freedom]]

## 摘要

内维尔·西明顿（Neville Symington）在《分析师的自由行动作为治疗性改变的动因》中考察了一个"所有分析师都熟悉"的现象，并命名为 **x 现象（x-phenomenon）**：**分析师身上一次内在自由行动（act of inner freedom），会引起病人的治疗性转变，并在分析师身上引发新的洞见、学习与发展**。

三项核心主张构成了这一概念的骨架。**其一，时序上的在先性**：真正的改变动因是分析师的"内在"行动，它先于洞见，也先于诠释；诠释是必要的，但其功能是**把已经发生的转变表达出来、使其可及于意识**。**其二，卷入是前提而非障碍**：分析师不断被"套索套进（lassoed into）"病人的投射系统与错觉世界，治疗行动不依赖于避免这种卷入（那是不可能的），而依赖于**在维持情感联结的同时从中获得片刻自由**。**其三，中立被重释**：这不是"不卷入的中立"，而是"**认出自己的卷入并从中获得片刻自由**"的中立。

核心临床范例是 M 小姐（Miss M.）案例：西明顿长期向她收取远低于常规的費用，突破来自一个不期而至的自发念头——"为什么 M 小姐不能付得和我的其他病人一样多？"。关联概念见 [[concepts/concept-corporate-personality]]；亚德林-加多特对同一案例的另一种解读见第十节。

## 一、x 现象的提出

> In this chapter I intend to explore a phenomenon with which all analysts are familiar. I will first describe it and then examine what its implications are for theory. I shall refer to it as the ‘x-phenomenon’

— Symington, *Freedom*，引论

> My contention is that the inner act of freedom in the analyst causes a therapeutic shift in the patient and new insight, learning and development in the analyst

— Symington, *Freedom*，「x 现象的界定」

作者强调这是对**分析师的**自由行动而非病人的自由行动的论断。

## 二、核心界定：内在行动先于并促成诠释，诠释只表达已发生的转变

这是全文最重要的一段界定：

> The interpretation is essential in that it gives *expression* to the shift that has already occurred and makes it available to consciousness. The point though is that the essential agent of change is the *inner* act of the analyst and that this inner act is perceived by the patient and causes change

— Symington, *Freedom*，「x 现象的界定」

> The sudden access of personal feeling in the analyst that breaks another bond of the illusory stranglehold in which both patient and analyst are held in thrall is immediately experienced by the patient and exists prior to insight

— Symington, *Freedom*，「理论建构」

在结论处，作者把这一层次结构说得更完整——**分析过程在前语言、原发过程层面运作，诠释是它的言语表达与最终完成**：

> The analytic process catalyses the individual to individual existent reality. The x-phenomenon is a product of the analytic process. The latter works at a deep level, at a pre-verbal, primary process level. It finds its verbal expression in interpretation. Interpretation expresses this deep change and effects the final consummation of it at the conscious and manifest level

— Symington, *Freedom*，「理论建构」

> The interpretations that follow the x-phenomenon become conscious articulations of a change that has already occurred unconsciously at the ego to ego level

— Symington, *Freedom*，「结论」

> The ‘x-phenomenon’ implies that there is a knowledge that is pre-verbal and that it is anterior to speech and therefore to interpret- ation. At this level of knowledge the patient knows unconsciously the analyst’s internal attitudes. If, for example, the analyst is uncon- sciously envious of the patient in some particular way then the patient perceives it and only a change in the analyst’s inner attitude will enable the patient to move forward psychically

— Symington, *Freedom*，「结论」

## 三、最内在的心理行动也有可被感知的外在对应物

作者主张精神、情绪与感觉领域"从不孤立存在"：

> I do not think that the mental, emotional and sensational spheres ever exist in isolation. The most inward mental act reverberates through the sensational and perceptual spheres. The psychotic patient is tuned in to these inner spheres in a way that is not so of neurotics or normal people

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三之后的理论过渡」

精神病患者不是"脱离现实"，而是现实的某一极小面向被放大——电视摄像机"变焦镜头"之喻：

> The psychotic is not cut off from reality but rather one minute aspect of reality is enlarged so that the rest of the mental or emotional field is crowded out. It is like the zoom lens on a television camera that swoops down on one object of interest and that one object then takes up the whole television screen

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三之后的理论过渡」

两个佐证片段。其一，病人的回应与他的情绪状态合拍，相较之下他的诠释"毫不重要"：

> ‘I am not taking stick for your bad experience.’ She was in tune with my emotional state in relation to which my interpretation bore little importance and she sensed this. She perceived it in the atmosphere. I am quite sure that she had no external knowledge of what had occurred

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三」

其二，一个纯内在的行政决定在同一瞬间被病人捕获：

> The moment I said to myself inwardly that I would make Tuesday the last session she said at that precise moment, ‘You have interrupted my thoughts, you have just stolen something from me.’ I had of course. Instead of being in reverie with her I had stolen a chunk of shared thinking in favour of an administrative decision. As far as she was concerned I might just as well have spoken my thoughts out loud because she felt my inner act so that even an inner judgement has some perceptible external correlate

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三」

## 四、M 小姐案例

**背景**：西明顿向 M 小姐收取"略高于其他病人一半"的费用；她曾是诊所病人。这一安排在心智中的地位被他比作英国天气的不可靠——是"我头脑里的家具"：

> I was charging Miss M. a little more than half what my other patients were paying. She had been a clinic patient and I used to sigh to myself and say inwardly, ‘Poor Miss M., £X is the most that I can charge her.’ I did not in fact articulate it so clearly as that. In my mind it was like an acknowledged fact that everyone knows, like the unreliability of the English weather. It was part of the furniture of my mind and I had resigned myself to it in the same way as I reluctantly resign myself to the English weather

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证一」

**自发的一念**：

> So the analysis went on and on with that assumption as its unquestioned concomitant until one day a startling thought occurred to me: ‘Why can’t Miss M. pay the same as all my other patients?’ Then I remembered the resentment she frequently expressed towards her boss who always called her *‘Little Mary’*.A certainty began to grow in me that I was the prisoner of an illusion about the patient’s capacities. I had been lassoed into the patient’s self-perception and I was just beginning to extricate myself from it

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证一」

**病人的回应与后续**：

> in the course of a discussion she said, ‘If I *had* to pay more then I know I would.’ She had now clearly told me that she had the capacity in her to pay more and that this could be mobilized if I changed my inner attitude towards her

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证一」

> A few sessions later I said to her, ‘I have been thinking over our discussion about the fee. I charge most of my other patients £X and in our discussion I have not heard anything that makes me think that I should not charge you the same.’ For two sessions she cried rather pitifully but then became resolved that she would meet the challenge. Soon she found a job that paid her one third more than her previous salary

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证一」

**因果判断**——外在一连串有益变化的源头被定位于那个内在自由的瞬间：

> She had been able to do this because she had first been freed from the patronizing attitude of her analyst

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证一」

> I think the source of these beneficial changes was in that moment of inner freedom when I had the unexpected thought: ‘Why can’t Miss M. pay the same as my other patients?’ I am calling this act of inner freedom the ‘x-phenomenon’

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证一」

## 五、其余临床例证

**（一）强迫症男性——"可我完全有自由那么想"**。西明顿读到比昂《与 W. R. 比昂的四次讨论》中的一段，获得"顿悟的时刻"：

> In view of what you are saying I think I would try to draw her attention to the way in which she wishes to limit my freedom about what I call her. It is just as much a limitation if the patient wants you to give the correct interpretation. Why shouldn’t I be free to form my own opinion that she’s a whore or that she is something quite different? Why be angry with me because in fact I am free to come to my own conclusions

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证二」（引 Bion, 1978, pp. 15–16）

> As I read this I had a moment of illumination about my obsessional patient. I had been a prisoner of this patient’s controlling impulses and at the moment of reading this passage from Bion I had a new understanding in which I felt freed inwardly (though this had outward concomitants). The next time he expressed his apprehension that I would think him pathetic I said to him quietly, ‘But I am quite free to think that.’ He was much taken aback

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证二」

后续的因果链条：控制他人想法 → 童年被寄养 → 思维能力的基础在于**母亲式欲望的内化**。

> The foundation of the thinking capacity seems to lie in the internalization of this maternal desire. Again the source of all this interpretative work and insight started from the moment of my own inner act of freedom

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证二」

**（二）幻觉性精神病女性**。约治疗第三年，病人开始坐到他身后的椅子上；他先是按既有做法诠释了约八次会谈，然后：

> Then one day I became uncomfortable with this procedure. I did not feel at ease and I was not able to respond spontaneously. Although I was interpreting, it was not out of an inner freedom but defensive in character. I decided that next time she walked past me and stationed herself behind my chair I would move across to the other side of the room. I could not say quite why I decided to do this but I knew that I could not interpret freely when I felt this discomfort

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三」

被指控"纯粹男性霸权"时，他体验到"一种此前未曾拥有的内在确信"：

> That composite moment when I acted and then when in response to her I experienced an inner certainty that I had not had before is another instance of the ‘x-phenomenon’

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三」

同一病人还有一段：她达到一个无法承受任何诠释的阶段，唯一可能的方式是在某些关头由他**表达他的感受是什么**；而这些沟通**本身就是诠释**：

> I say ‘in the normal way’ because I think the communications about my feelings were interpretations. They were interpretations about the way she was merged with me through the superego structure of her personality

— Symington, *Freedom*，「临床例证三」

**（三）"我不动，你就不动"**。一位女病人反复说这句话，约三年后他才明白其意：

> She often said that she could not move until I moved first. For a very long time I did not understand this. Only after about three years of treatment I suddenly realized that she meant that she could only move when an inner act of freedom had occurred within me

— Symington, *Freedom*，「理论建构」

> She was reliant on x-phenomenon but for a long time she had the fantasy that it was within my power to summon it at will. She became sad as it began to dawn on her that I had to wait, just as she did

— Symington, *Freedom*，「理论建构」

## 六、中立的重释：不是不卷入，而是认出卷入并从中获得片刻自由

这是戈夫林在《诠释》第七章第二节对西明顿的浓缩转述：

> Symington’s 1983 paper introduces what he calls the “x-phenomenon”—the analyst’s internal act of freedom that precedes and enables meaningful interpretation (Symington, 1983, p. 283). This is the freedom to think one’s own thoughts while remaining emotionally engaged with the patient. It reflects the capacity to be both inside and outside the patient’s projective system simultaneously—a paradox that defines the analyst’s impossible but necessary position

— Govrin, *Interpretation*，第七章第二节「转向主体性：内在自由作为治疗行动」

> What Symington discovered was that analysts are constantly being “lassoed into” their patients’ projective systems (Symington, 1983, p. 283). We become unwitting actors in our patients’ internal dramas, playing roles we don’t consciously choose. The therapeutic action depends not on avoiding this entanglement—which is impossible—but on achieving moments of freedom from it while maintaining emotional connection

— Govrin, *Interpretation*，第七章第二节

> This is a different kind of neutrality than classical analysis imagined: not the neutrality of non-involvement but the neutrality that comes from recognizing one’s involvement and achieving momentary freedom from it

— Govrin, *Interpretation*，第七章第二节

戈夫林指出，这一转向"**从技术转向了分析师的心智状态**"，并"最终导向关系革命"。

## 七、与投射性认同划界：第三词项是「过程」

西明顿预先回应了一种可能的归类，并拒绝之：

> It could be argued that what I am describing is a particular instance of projective identification but I do not think this does justice to those psychological events which, for want of a better term, I have called the x-phenomenon. Projective identification means that feelings which belong to the patient are projected into the analyst and lodge there like a foreign body. What I am describing is a joint process in which the real feelings of analyst and patient are aroused by the resistant process

— Symington, *Freedom*，「批评投射性认同」

> The analyst’s feelings are *his* feelings even though they may have been stirred up by the patient. Patient and analyst are responsible for the feelings that are generated in the situation. Often the patient is ‘blamed’ for feelings experienced by the analyst and this is called projective identification. This type of description implies that there are only two blameable objects in the room: patient and analyst. There is a third term: the process in which both are involved

— Symington, *Freedom*，「批评投射性认同」

## 八、与「共同人格」的关联

x 现象的理论支撑正是**团体性人格（corporate personality）**模型：分析师与病人经由双方**超我**融合为一个共同实体，分析师的个人感受被源自病人无意识超我的错觉性感受所遮蔽。

> In all the instances of x-phenomenon that I have given, the analyst’s personal feelings have been shrouded by illusory feelings, emanating from the patient’s unconscious superego. This could be formulated by saying that the feelings belonging to the superego have cloaked the feelings belonging to the ego

— Symington, *Freedom*，「理论建构」

因此，病人的分离需要触及分析师的**核心感受（core feelings）**：

> In order to separate, the patient needs to get access to the analyst’s core feelings. His interpretations need to flow from here to as great an extent as possible if the patient is to be able to separate

— Symington, *Freedom*，「洞见行动」

> My greatest problem in his analysis was to reach those feelings which were most truly mine. In the case of that patient the problem was particularly acute but on reflection I think this may be a central problem in every analysis

— Symington, *Freedom*，「洞见行动」

完整论述见 [[concepts/concept-corporate-personality]]。

## 九、认识论辩护：一种不经由感官、也不经由意识的特殊知识

西明顿承认自己的说法"看似与弗洛伊德的观点相矛盾"，随后给出两段弗洛伊德引文作为反证：

> I have had good reason for asserting that everyone possesses in his own unconscious an instrument with which he can interpret the utterances of the unconscious in other people. (1913i, p. 320)

— Symington, *Freedom*，「认识论辩护」（引 Freud, 1913i）

> It is a very remarkable thing that the *Ucs*. of one human being can react upon that of another, without passing through the *Cs*.This deserves closer investigation, especially with a view to finding out whether preconscious activity can be excluded as playing a part in it; but, descriptively speaking, the fact is incontestable. (1915e, p. 194)

— Symington, *Freedom*，「认识论辩护」（引 Freud, 1915e）

> He is here talking about a special type of knowledge that exists between human beings that does not pass through the normal sense organs or through that conscious part of the personality inhabited by word-presentations. This particular type of knowledge therefore antedates any interpretation that the analyst may give

— Symington, *Freedom*，「认识论辩护」

这一特殊知识的谱系被追溯到维柯（Giambattista Vico）与韦伯（Max Weber）：

> He distinguished between the ordinary knowledge by which we know the physical universe which he called *Wissen* and that special type of knowledge proper only to the knowledge of human beings by human beings and this he called *Verstehen*. Although this special type of knowledge has been central to clinical work in psychoanalytic practice there does not seem to be a metapsychology to account for it

— Symington, *Freedom*，「认识论辩护」

洞见的行动本身被借康德阅读经验加以刻画——**理解之行动植根于最具个人性的自我，错误观念位于超我；洞见时刻二者同时被消解**：

> At the moment of understanding I become Kant, as it were, through an action of the ego, whereby I dispel my superego contents and because of this I remain separated and become slightly more of an individual. At the moment of understanding activity and passivity come together and form a single psychological event

— Symington, *Freedom*，「认识论辩护」

> The act of understanding is rooted in what is most personal, in the ego, but the false ideas are located in the superego. At the moment of insight, expressed in interpretation, the illusions or false ideas are banished both in analyst and in patient

— Symington, *Freedom*，「洞见行动」

## 十、亚德林-加多特的另一种解读

亚德林-加多特把同一案例读作**以对应真理解构一个根深蒂固的实用真理**，并承认这可能把"平淡、清醒的对应"引入我们深爱的诗学领域（见 [[concepts/concept-truth-axes]] 8.2）：

> Symington suggests an elaborate explanation for the “x-phenom- enon” involving unconscious communication and an illusory corporate personality system shared by patient and analyst and dominated by elements of superego. The analyst’s act of freedom brings to consciousness the imperatives of this system and the ability to question them

— Yadlin-Gadot, *Truth Axes*，「自由行动」

> The case cited in Symington’s (1983) “The Analyst’s Act of Free- dom as Agent of Therapeutic Change” illustrates once again the therapeutic effect involved in movement among truth axes, trig- gered by the analyst’s intervention. The case illustrates the po- tency of deconstructing an entrenched, Pragmatic truth by means of Correspondence

— Yadlin-Gadot, *Truth Axes*，「自由行动」

> Symington’s moment of freedom involved a Correspondently determined thought that entered his mind: “Why can’t Miss M pay the same as all my other patients?” This thought was “startling” for the analyst, not a part of the “furniture of his mind.” This is mostly because the Correspondent axis was not the one structur- ing the reality of the analytic dyad

— Yadlin-Gadot, *Truth Axes*，「自由行动」

> ⚠️ **矛盾标注**
> **议题**：x 现象的治疗机制如何解释？
> **A 方立场**：西明顿本人——诉诸无意识沟通，以及一个由病人与分析者共享、被超我元素支配的"虚幻的法人格系统"；分析者的自由行动使这一系统的命令进入意识并变得可被质疑。
> **B 方立场**：亚德林-加多特——提出"另一种理解"，主张该案例显示的是**以对应真理解构一个根深蒂固的实用真理**的力量，即轴间运动被分析者干预所触发；她并指出，用真理术语分析自由时刻有把"平淡、清醒的对应"引入诗学领域的风险。
> **备注**：可调和且互补。西明顿解释的是**机制**（为什么内在自由能传达），亚德林-加多特解释的是**内容**（那一个念头属于哪一条真理轴，为何具有解构力）。两者在西明顿文本内部并不冲突——"共同人格"与"对应真理"分别回答不同层次的问题。
